The question

Chapters 2–4 built a reconstruction step by step: relative poses from F/E, points from triangulation. Each step uses only part of the data, so errors pile up. Bundle adjustment (BA) refines all cameras and all points together so that the reprojected points match the detections as well as possible. The name comes from the "bundles" of rays leaving each camera, adjusted until they agree. Every SfM / SLAM system (COLMAP, ORB-SLAM, …) ends with it.

1. The cost

residualrᵢⱼ = π(Kᵢ (Rᵢ Xⱼ + tᵢ)) − xᵢⱼ (2 numbers, pixels)
costE(θ) = Σᵢⱼ ‖rᵢⱼ‖², θ = (R₁, t₁, …, Rₘ, tₘ, X₁, …, Xₙ)

This is non-linear least squares in 6m + 3n unknowns. Rotations are updated on the manifold with a small axis-angle vector: R ← exp([δω]×) R. Under Gaussian pixel noise its minimum is the maximum-likelihood reconstruction.

2. Gauss–Newton and Levenberg–Marquardt

Linearize the residuals around the current estimate, r(θ + δ) ≈ r + J δ, and minimize the quadratic:

Gauss–Newton(JᵀJ) δ = −Jᵀ r
Levenberg–Marquardt(JᵀJ + λ diag(JᵀJ)) δ = −Jᵀ r

Gauss–Newton converges very fast near the solution but can overshoot from a bad start. LM adds damping λ: if a step increases the cost it is rejected and λ grows (shorter, gradient-descent-like steps); if it succeeds λ shrinks (towards Gauss–Newton). The Jacobian here uses finite differences; real solvers (Ceres, g2o, GTSAM) use analytic or automatic derivatives.

3. Sparsity and the Schur complement

Each residual depends on one camera and one point only, so JᵀJ is mostly zeros, with an "arrowhead" pattern: a small camera block, a big block-diagonal point block, and coupling blocks. Eliminating the points first (Schur complement) leaves a system of size 6m instead of 6m + 3n — the reason BA scales to millions of points.

4. Gauge freedom

Images do not tell where the world origin is, how it is oriented, or how big the scene is. Rotating, translating or scaling everything together leaves every residual unchanged: 7 degrees of freedom (a similarity) that the cost cannot see. JᵀJ is singular along them. Fixing camera 1 removes 6; the remaining scale is kept in check by the LM damping. Errors to the ground truth are therefore measured after aligning with the best similarity transform.

5. Things to try

  • Press Run and watch the red residual vectors shrink in each image while the cameras slide into place in 3D.
  • Set σ to 0: the cost goes to essentially 0. With noise it stops at about σ — the reconstruction is as good as the data allows.
  • Increase the initial rotation error to 8–10° and try Gauss–Newton vs LM.
  • Uncheck fix camera 1: camera 1 is optimized too. The cost reaches the same minimum — it cannot see the 7 gauge directions — which is why errors are compared after a similarity alignment.
